## 1. Luxury / integrator systems (Josh.ai, Control4 X4, Crestron Home OS 4, Savant, Basalte, Lutron, Brilliant, UniFi): signature UI ideas

### Takeaway
The integrator platforms have converged on a customisable home page of pinned favourites plus a live "what is active right now" strip (lights on, media sessions), rooms as the main navigation unit, homeowner-editable routines with a visible timeline, and natural-language scene generation. The single most distinctive visual idea is still Savant's TrueImage, a photograph of the room that brightens and tints live as you drag a slider, and it is reproducible on the web with two photos per fixture.

### Inferences
- **"Active now" beats "everything".** Josh (lights currently on) and Control4 (active media sessions) both lead the home screen with what is running, not the device inventory. For a web app: a top strip listing only active things, each with a one-tap off and a tap-through to its room.
- **One slider per level of the hierarchy** (house, room, light) is the cleanest answer to mixed-state groups, and it matches the finding that people control rooms, not bulbs.
- **TrueImage is web-reproducible with no special hardware** beyond a phone camera at capture time: store an off photo and an on photo per fixture (or per room), cross-fade by brightness, and tint for colour temperature; tap regions toggle. The capture discipline matters more than the code (fixed framing, screens off, enough contrast between the two frames).
- **Routine Timeline** ("what will the house do next") is a trust feature worth borrowing for a home with alarm arming, borehole pumping and battery schedules.
- **Describe-a-mood scenes** only need a language model plus the device list; the "suggest scenes from the devices you actually have" framing is the borrowable part.
- **CVD:** integrator lighting UIs lean on hue wheels and colour swatches; Savant's approach of showing the actual lit room sidesteps colour naming entirely.

**Apple: Liquid Glass (iOS 26 family) [SHIPPED]**
- Defined as a "digital meta-material that dynamically bends and shapes light"; its signature is *lensing* (bending and concentrating light) instead of blur-scatter; elements appear and disappear by modulating lensing, not by fading — [Apple WWDC25 session 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/).
- Depth: larger elements (menus) simulate thicker glass with deeper shadows and stronger refraction; shadow opacity rises over text and falls over plain light backgrounds — [Apple WWDC25 session 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/).
- Motion: glass responds to touch by "instantly flexing and energizing with light" with gel-like elasticity; controls can lift into glass only during interaction so the resting state stays quiet; menus open in place from the button that triggered them — [Apple WWDC25 session 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/).
- Adaptivity: small elements (nav bars, tab bars, glyphs) flip light/dark based on what is behind them; large elements (menus, sidebars) adapt but do not flip because the transition would be uncomfortable over a large area — [Apple WWDC25 session 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/).
- Rules: reserve glass for the *navigation layer* floating above content, never the content layer; avoid glass on glass; tint selectively for primary actions only and "put color in the content layer"; use scroll-edge effects so content dissolves under floating bars; two variants (Regular, Clear) that must not be mixed — [Apple WWDC25 session 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/).
- Accessibility switches: Reduced Transparency makes glass frostier; Increased Contrast makes elements predominantly black or white with a contrasting border; Reduced Motion disables the elastic properties — [Apple WWDC25 session 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/).
- Criticism: reviewers report poor legibility in translucent areas and one informal test measured menu text at 3.8:1 contrast, below the 4.5:1 guideline (snippet; single informal test) — [Infinum](https://infinum.com/blog/apples-ios-26-liquid-glass-sleek-shiny-and-questionably-accessible/), [Pixel Envy](https://pxlnv.com/blog/on-liquid-glass/), [OSnews](https://www.osnews.com/story/143522/liquid-glass-is-cracked-and-usability-suffers-in-ios-26/). A counter-view argues it is Apple's "most tactile digital future yet" (snippet) — [UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/liquid-glass-isnt-a-design-failure-it-is-apple-s-most-tactile-digital-future-yet-091abade02b3).

### Inferences
- The shared pattern is a **three-part home screen**: a generated sentence or brief ("what happened / what matters"), a natural-language input, and a small set of modes and favourites. Device lists are demoted to a second level.
- **Home / Away / Night as one-tap modes** (Amazon) maps directly onto a household with a perimeter alarm; it is the highest-value "intent" control for that home.
- **Home Brief** is implementable on the web as a daily digest card: a few ranked events with thumbnails, adjustable focus (kids, gate, pets, energy), and follow-up questions.
- **Proximity adaptation** needs sensing the browser does not have. A web equivalent can use what a home platform already knows (a motion or presence sensor near the wall display, time since last touch) to switch between a far "ambient" layout and a near "control" layout; per-person recognition would have to come from presence data or an explicit profile tap, not the screen itself.
- Liquid Glass rules translate cleanly to CSS-era thinking even without the material: floating navigation over content, tint only the primary action, never stack translucent layers, and provide reduced-transparency / increased-contrast / reduced-motion variants. **CVD:** the legibility complaints are a warning against putting status text on translucent surfaces over photos.
- "Faces" (rumoured) suggests treating the always-on display as a small set of user-selectable whole-screen compositions, not a configurable grid.

## 3. Ambient and glanceable displays: calm technology, density, motion, night-time, burn-in, attention

### Takeaway
The consistent advice is that an always-on screen in a living space should be a status board, not a control panel: one to a few pieces of information, refreshed at the rate the information actually changes, with items appearing only when relevant and no constantly changing ticker. Night dimming, slight pixel shifting, avoiding large bright static areas and presence-based sleep are the standard always-on mitigations; status must never rely on hue alone.

### Cited Findings

**Calm technology**
- The Calm Tech Institute's eight principles: (1) require the smallest possible amount of attention; (2) inform and create calm; (3) make use of the periphery; (4) amplify the best of technology and the best of humanity; (5) communicate without speaking; (6) still work when it fails; (7) the minimum tech needed to solve the problem; (8) respect social norms — [Calm Tech Institute](https://www.calmtech.institute/calm-tech-principles).
- Calm Tech Certified is described as a standard built on "periphery engagement, tactility, material harmony and durability" — [Calm Tech Institute](https://www.calmtech.institute/calm-tech-principles). Reporting describes 81 points across six categories: attention, periphery, durability, light, sound and materials (snippet) — [Slashdot/IEEE Spectrum summary](https://tech.slashdot.org/story/25/01/22/056208/calm-tech-certification-rewards-less-distracting-tech).
- A well-designed technology should "move easily from the periphery of our attention, to the center, and back"; touch interfaces without tactile feedback increase cognitive load and require visual attention (snippet) — [Wikipedia: Calm technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calm_technology), [Calm Tech Institute](https://www.calmtech.institute/post/your-hands-have-eyes-too-engaging-peripheral-attention).
- The Mui Board Gen 2 is Calm Tech Certified: a wooden panel whose dot-matrix display stays hidden in the wood and "softly appears only when needed"; around $999 (snippet) — [Design Compass](https://designcompass.org/en/2026/01/20/mui-board-2nd-generation-a-calm-and-comfortable-smart-home-interface/), [mui Lab](https://muilab.com/products_and_services/muiboard).

**Research framing**
- Ambient displays typically communicate "just one, or perhaps a few at the most, pieces of information", with aesthetics often paramount; Pousman and Stasko's taxonomy uses four dimensions: information capacity, notification level, representational fidelity and aesthetic emphasis (snippet) — [Pousman & Stasko, AVI 2006](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~john.stasko/papers/avi06.pdf).
- Glanceability is defined as enabling users to understand information with low cognitive effort, for divided-attention situations (snippet) — [Matthews, UC Berkeley tech report 2006](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/2006/EECS-2006-113.pdf).

**What practitioners put on always-on screens**
- TRMNL (7.5-inch 800x480 e-ink) review: "It's a status board, not a control panel"; show curated glanceable information instead of another screen competing for attention; hourly refresh is right because "this isn't stock trading or server monitoring"; match refresh frequency to how fast the information changes. Family effect: kids who rarely opened the phone calendar now see the schedule on the wall several times a day — [HomeTechHacker](https://hometechhacker.com/trmnl-og-e-ink-dashboard-review-a-focused-battery-powered-family-status-board/).
- E-ink only draws power when the image changes and is pitched as "ambient, glanceable updates without the harsh glare of a traditional screen"; refreshes cause a visible flash (snippet) — [SparkFun](https://www.sparkfun.com/trmnl-e-ink-dashboard-7-5-inch.html).
- Home Assistant community thread (2026) on wall tablets: the original poster argues most people only want to know what is wrong, coming up, finished or needs action, and built a notification layer that surfaces those, opening deeper dashboards only when needed. Replies describe a hallway display with weather/radar, a lightning card only during nearby storms, battery gauges, a doorbell-camera pop-up, and icons that appear *only when relevant* (bins, finished appliances, parcels, leak alerts), with no light controls; and a room display that switches to media info when music plays — [Home Assistant Community](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/are-we-thinking-about-wall-tablets-the-wrong-way/1019690).
- Same thread, cautions: spoken announcements annoy (the author dropped most of them so the house "would stop yelling at everyone"); several households say nobody but the builder looks at the wall screen — [Home Assistant Community](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/are-we-thinking-about-wall-tablets-the-wrong-way/1019690).
- Pixel displays: the Ulanzi TC001 is a 32x8 RGB LED matrix that, with AWTRIX firmware, shows one item at a time (text plus small icon) and cycles through items at a configurable interval; examples include current solar yield in watts; brightness can be set to 0 at night by automation, or auto-adjusted from its light sensor (snippet) — [raspberry.tips](https://raspberry.tips/en/raspberrypi-tutorials/ulanzi-tc001-awtrix-home-assistant), [New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/consumer-tech/ulanzi-tc001-pixel-clock-smart-home/).
- "Data as art" / art-mode screens: Samsung The Frame's Art Mode adjusts brightness and colour tone to room lighting and uses a **HW:** motion sensor to turn the display on or off with movement; a matte panel matters because glare ruins the effect in a bright room (snippet) — [Samsung support](https://www.samsung.com/ca/support/tv-audio-video/art-mode-on-your-samsung-the-frame-tv/), [Livingetc](https://www.livingetc.com/us/advice/samsung-frame-alternatives).
- Negative example: a reviewer of the Echo Show 21 "did hate the 'news ticker' type approach that was always changing", and Consumer Reports warns its entertainment features can distract (snippet; affiliate-style roundups) — [wetried.it](https://wetried.it/best-smart-display), [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/electronics-computers/best-digital-calendars-a1935397155/).
- Skylight Calendar Max (27-inch) "doubles as a photo frame when idle" (snippet) — [wetried.it](https://wetried.it/compare/skylight-calendar-max-vs-hearth-display/).

**Night-time and burn-in**
- Android's always-on guidance: when burn-in protection is required, "periodically shift the UI elements slightly and avoid solid white areas" (snippet) — [Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/training/wearables/views/always-on).
- A watch maker describes the same: the time "moves around slightly to prevent pixel burn-in" (snippet) — [Polar](https://support.polar.com/e_manuals/ignite-3/polar-ignite-3-user-manual-english/backlight-and-display-settings.htm).
- Brightness is usually driven from ambient light in lux bands (dark room, dim room, office, daylight), allowing large backlight cuts at night (snippet) — [DigiKey](https://www.digikey.nl/nl/articles/how-to-optimize-display-brightness-for-low-power-in-real-time).

**Colour-independent status (CVD)**
- Redundant encoding: every piece of information carried by colour must also be carried by at least one colour-independent channel (shape, pattern, position, text, icon or luminance contrast), per WCAG 1.4.1; red/green status indicators and red-to-green gradients fail without it (snippet) — [UI/UX Atlas](https://uiux-atlas.pages.dev/lessons/color/color-blindness-and-inclusive-color-design).
- Use distinct icon shapes per state (check for normal, exclamation for warning, X for outage); do not use icons that only change colour while keeping the same shape; avoid bare status dots; avoid blinking or moving objects for status (snippet) — [Oracle accessibility guidance](https://docs.oracle.com/cd/G10809_01/pt861pbr4/eng/pt/tacs/ProvidingAlternativestoColorCodingtoConveyMeaning-ba7fc3.html), [Inclusive Android Apps](https://buttondown.com/inclusive-android-apps/archive/inclusive-android-apps-3-the-problem-of-color-as-the-only-indicator).
- Palette: shift away from pure red/green toward bluish-green for success and orange-red for errors; if two states share a shape they should differ in luminance, not just hue; simulate deuteranopia in design review (snippet) — [UI/UX Atlas](https://uiux-atlas.pages.dev/lessons/color/color-blindness-and-inclusive-color-design), [TPGi](https://www.tpgi.com/colour-deficit-help).

### Inferences
- A practical density rule drawn from the above: an always-on family display should carry one dominant element (clock, photo or art), a handful of persistent facts at most, and an "exceptions" zone that is empty when all is well. Presence of an item is itself the signal, which is also colour-independent.
- Motion budget: slow, low-contrast change only (cross-fades on a timescale of minutes, e-ink-like cadence); reserve any faster motion for a real event (doorbell, alarm), and let it settle. Tickers and auto-rotating carousels are the named anti-pattern.
- "Periphery to centre and back" translates to three states of the same screen: ambient (far, quiet), glance (something relevant appears), control (someone touches it), with automatic return to ambient.
- For a TV/wall display that is an emissive panel: dim with ambient light or time of day, go near-black at night, shift layout by a few pixels periodically, avoid large static white fields, and blank on absence if a presence signal is available. E-ink or pixel-matrix thinking (one fact at a time) is a useful constraint even on a 4K TV.
- **CVD:** for alarm, battery and water status use shape + label + position first (shield with tick / shield with exclamation, a filled-level bar with a number), luminance second, hue last; never a red/green dot.

### Gaps
- No published Nest Hub "Ambient EQ" design guidance was found; nothing specific on DAKboard, MagicMirror, Glance, Inkplate or reTerminal E design conventions; Tidbyt's current status was not sourced.
- No quantitative research found (in this pass) on maximum item counts, viewing-distance type sizes, or motion thresholds for domestic always-on displays; the density guidance above is practitioner opinion plus 2006-era taxonomy.
- Nothing found on children's attention specifically around always-on home displays.

## 5. Tangible and physical controls, and on-screen mimicry

### Takeaway
There is a steady stream of dial-and-button products (Homey Portal, Flic Twist, SwitchBot Hub 3, Josh Edge, Mui Board) built on the argument that walking up and twisting something beats unlocking a phone, and calm-tech guidance explicitly penalises touch surfaces with no tactile feedback. - DIY: an open-source ESP32 rotary-encoder controller was built by a Home Assistant user "for my family who hated touchscreens" (title only) — [Home Assistant Community](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/i-built-an-open-source-controller-for-my-family-who-hated-touchscreens-esp32-s3-rotary-encoder/972165); a circular dial HMI project for Home Assistant (title only) — [Hackster](https://www.hackster.io/JasonFang/smart-home-button-a-circular-hmi-for-home-assistant-872133).

- On-screen tactility in Liquid Glass: controls flex like gel under the finger, lift into the material only while being manipulated, and glow from within on interaction; Reduced Motion turns the elasticity off — [Apple WWDC25 session 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/).

### Inferences
- The shared hardware grammar is: **tap = select/toggle, twist = adjust the most relevant continuous value, swipe = next/previous**. An on-screen equivalent is a single large dial or full-width slider per room that binds to whatever is contextually primary (light level by default, volume when media plays, setpoint when climate is focused), echoing Josh Edge's context switch.
- Coupled controls (Warm Glow) reduce two sliders to one and feel physical; the same idea applies to "evening" dimming where brightness and warmth move together.
- A context-following dial is hardware-neutral on screen; real detents and haptics are not available in desktop browsers and only partly on phones, so visual and audio "ticks" and snap points have to carry the feel.
- Cheap physical buttons and dials can sit alongside the web UI as first-class inputs; the web UI should show what a nearby physical control currently does.

### Inferences
- Three layers recur and stack well: (1) a **sentence** ("running on solar, battery full by early afternoon"), (2) a **live flow diagram** with 4-5 nodes, (3) **history/attribution** (Sankey, per-circuit cost). Lunar's "just what you need" and Tesla's AI status both push layer 1 to the front.
- The flow diagram earns its popularity because direction and magnitude are encoded by motion and position, not only hue. **CVD:** Tesla's yellow/green/grey and SolarEdge's "green means self-powered" lean on hue; for a red-green colour-blind user encode source by fixed node position and icon, direction by arrowheads or moving dashes, magnitude by line thickness and a number, and battery state by a fill level plus percentage.
- Water fits the same grammar: HA's water Sankey shows a borehole-tank-house-irrigation chain can be drawn as a parallel flow diagram beside electricity.
- The HA 2026.3 backlash is evidence that gauges alone feel like a downgrade from an animated flow; keep the flow as the hero and let gauges be secondary.
- "Backup time remaining" (Lunar) and battery state-of-charge badge (HA 2026.6) are the two numbers worth making glanceable on an always-on display for a solar + battery home.
- Sign conventions should be explicit in labels ("charging", "exporting") instead of plus/minus.

## 7. Family-oriented home screens: chores, calendars, routines, per-person personalisation

### Takeaway
Dedicated family displays treat the calendar as the calm idle state and layer kid-facing routines on top: icon-based steps a non-reader can follow one card at a time, streaks and stars, with parents editing from a phone. Per-person adaptation on shipped products comes from camera recognition (Amazon Visual ID) or explicit profiles; Apple's version is still rumour.

### Cited Findings
- **Hearth Display** [SHIPPED]: routines replace printed charts. Two views: **Card View** shows one step at a time for focused completion; **List View** gives the overview. Visual progress indicators; icons represent tasks so younger children can complete routines with less guidance; templates grouped by age range; steps can change by day of week; completing a routine on consecutive days shows a streak icon on the Routines card; each family member's progress is visible on their profile; parents create and edit in the companion app and can be notified when a routine starts or finishes — [Hearth](https://hearthdisplay.com/blogs/hearth-at-home/the-magic-of-hearth-s-routines).
- Hearth "Stars & Rewards": parents track progress, award stars and approve rewards from a phone; routines, rewards, lists, meal planning, "Today's Feelings" and the AI "Hearth Helper" need a paid membership, while calendar, colour coding and unlimited profiles keep working without it (snippet) — [Hearth](https://hearthdisplay.com/blogs/hearth-at-home/introducing-stars-rewards-big-cheers-for-little-wins), [Hearth membership](https://hearthdisplay.com/products/family-membership).
- **Skylight Calendar** [SHIPPED]: the chore chart lives in a Tasks tab; a task is either a Chore or a Routine and can be converted; assigning stars and creating Rewards needs the Plus subscription; kid-friendly icons such as a backpack or toothbrush make routines followable before children can read (snippet) — [Skylight](https://au.myskylight.com/how-to-manage-chores-and-family-tasks-with-skylight-calendar), [The Quality Edit](https://www.thequalityedit.com/articles/hearth-vs-skylight-review).
- Skylight Calendar Max is a 27-inch panel that doubles as a photo frame when idle; reviewers split Hearth (strongest for chores and routines) from Skylight (calm glanceable calendar) (snippet; affiliate-style reviews) — [wetried.it](https://wetried.it/compare/skylight-calendar-max-vs-hearth-display/).
- **Amazon Echo Show** [SHIPPED]: colour-coded family calendars with a widget that switches day/week/month, to-dos assignable to family members, Alexa+ summarising the day and suggesting times; **HW:** the camera recognises a person walking up, greets them and shows their information — [About Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/amazon-new-echo-devices-alexa-plus), [About Amazon (getting started)](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/getting-started-echo-show-8-11-alexa-plus-features).
- Echo Show 15 (2021 launch) introduced the widget home screen (shared calendar, to-do lists, sticky notes, package tracking) and visual ID to personalise on-screen information (snippet) — [About Amazon](https://aboutamazon.com/news/devices/alexa-keeps-getting-smarter).
- **Google** [SHIPPED]: Gemini uses saved household details (names, tagged faces) to answer "did X arrive" questions, and camera AI features are restricted to adult Admins — [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/05/11/google-home-gemini-speed-ask-home-voice-updates/), [Google support](https://support.google.com/googlehome/answer/15542305).
- **Apple** [RUMOUR]: the home hub is reported to use face and voice recognition to personalise content per person (snippet) — [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/home/smart-home/apple-announces-welcome-home-event-for-oct-13-homepad-and-all-the-new-products-to-expect).
- Wall placement effect: with the schedule on an always-on wall display, children who ignored the phone calendar now see it several times a day — [HomeTechHacker](https://hometechhacker.com/trmnl-og-e-ink-dashboard-review-a-focused-battery-powered-family-status-board/).
- Community proposal: let each person subscribe to what they want to be told about, with parents able to override and assign chore lists or maintenance to a specific person — [Home Assistant Community](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/are-we-thinking-about-wall-tablets-the-wrong-way/1019690).

### Inferences
- The borrowable kid pattern is **one big step at a time**: large icon, short label, a single large "done" target, visible progress, a small celebration, then the next card. It needs no reading and no navigation.
- Routines are **time-windowed** (morning, after school, bedtime) and vary by weekday, so the family display can surface the relevant routine automatically and hide it otherwise, which fits the "appears only when relevant" ambient rule.
- Parent-edits-on-phone / child-uses-on-wall is a role split worth copying across the phone and wall-display form factors.
- Per-person adaptation without camera hardware: a row of large profile avatars on the wall display (tap your face), or presence data from the home platform. **HW:** automatic walk-up recognition needs a camera or similar sensor on the display.
- **CVD:** every product here uses colour-per-person calendars. Pair each person's colour with an avatar, initial or icon, and choose person colours that differ in lightness, not just red/green hue.
- Access control matters in a home with cameras and an alarm: Google's adult-Admin restriction is a precedent for hiding camera history and arming controls from child profiles.

### Gaps
- Nothing reliable found on Cozyla's interface (only a vendor comparison page) or on 2026 Echo Show 15/21 family features; no confirmation of Apple family features.
- Most Hearth/Skylight comparisons are affiliate or vendor content; I found no independent usability evaluation of kids' routine UIs.
- No sources found on kid-safe control design (what children should and should not be able to operate on a shared wall display).

## 8. Design critique and principles: what is wrong with tile-grid dashboards and what replaces them

### Takeaway
The long-standing critique is that tile grids mirror how the system stores devices instead of what people are trying to do, making states hard to scan and hierarchies invisible. The proposed replacements cluster around five ideas: start from intent, organise by room, surface only what needs attention, layer detail progressively, and prefer automation or a physical control over a screen where possible.

### Cited Findings
- **Uniform tiles**: in Apple's Home app "everything is represented by the same gray square or rectangular tiles", a design that "makes device buttons nearly impossible to distinguish from one another at a glance"; off devices are grey on grey, names truncate at roughly 9-10 characters, and the app "obscures spatial and device hierarchies"; reaching settings takes "more tapping and swiping than most apps". Proposed: reflect the hierarchy of spaces and accessories, make accessories distinguishable at a glance, add state awareness and timers to scenes (article circa 2018; foundational critique) — [MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/stories/its-time-for-a-complete-home-app-makeover/).
- **Backend-shaped dashboards**: backend data is organised by integration and device type because that is how the system is built, and first-draft home dashboards inherit that structure; organise by room in almost every case because people think spatially about their homes (snippet; attribution to this article is from a search summary) — [Art of Styleframe](https://artofstyleframe.com/blog/smart-home-dashboard-ui-design/).
- **Intent over device structure**: the Daedalus Dashboard framework for Home Assistant replaces "integrations, entities, or rooms" as the organising idea with the question "What is the user trying to do right now?", plus semantic consistency, a fixed four-level navigation hierarchy and context-aware layouts, aiming for use "without training" — [Home Assistant Community](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/daedalus-dashboard-project/1008399).
- **Start from what you want to control**: Home Assistant 2026.6's card picker opens on a "By entity" tab so you "start from what you want to control, and let Home Assistant suggest the rest"; weather tiles add forecasts so the dashboard "answers the next question before you ask it" — [Home Assistant 2026.6](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2026/06/03/release-20266/).
- **Room-first default**: Home Assistant 2025.12 made "Home" a built-in dashboard (the Areas dashboard "evolved into Home"), added a sidebar of quick-access links, and lets users drag to reorder floors and areas with the order applied across Home, Lights and Security dashboards — [Home Assistant 2025.12](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2025/12/03/release-202512/).
- **Dashboards are not the primary interface**: "Why do we still build dashboards like they're the primary interface to our homes?" Most people want to know whether something is wrong, coming up, finished or needs action. Counter-positions in the same thread: "If you need to touch a screen, you're doing it all wrong" (automation and voice first); dashboards are for admins only; others insist on direct manual control and distrust AI — [Home Assistant Community](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/are-we-thinking-about-wall-tablets-the-wrong-way/1019690).
- **Two user mindsets**: a Home Assistant developer says roughly half of users think "task based" and half "device/entity based" (snippet; attribution from search summary) — [Home Assistant Community](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/future-prototype-of-home-assistant/488558).
- **Rooms, not bulbs; status versus intent**: Josh.ai's research found users mostly control whole rooms, and that a control must make both current state and the effect of a tap clear — [Josh.ai](https://josh.ai/stories/the-hard-thing-about-designing-the-smart-home-lighting-control).
- **Insights replace favourites**: Samsung replaced the Favourites tab with a Home tab of personalised Insight messages (snippet) — [Samsung Newsroom](https://news.samsung.com/us/samsung-making-home-better-connected-major-smartthings-updates/).
- **Calm-tech principles** applicable to home UIs: smallest possible attention, use the periphery, communicate without speaking, still work when it fails, minimum tech needed — [Calm Tech Institute](https://www.calmtech.institute/calm-tech-principles).
- **Status board, not control panel** for always-on screens — [HomeTechHacker](https://hometechhacker.com/trmnl-og-e-ink-dashboard-review-a-focused-battery-powered-family-status-board/).
- **Friction argument for physical controls**: "Turning on a light shouldn't require unlocking a phone, opening an app, and tapping through menus" — [Yanko Design](https://www.yankodesign.com/2026/09/03/finally-a-smart-home-you-can-control-without-your-phone/).
- **Layering rule from Liquid Glass**: keep the floating navigation layer distinct from content, do not tint everything, and put colour in the content layer — [Apple WWDC25 session 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/).

### Inferences
- A workable synthesis for a bespoke family interface is a **glance, control, detail** ladder: (glance) a sentence and an exceptions list that is empty when all is well; (control) room-level intents and house modes; (detail) individual devices, history and settings one level down. I did not find this exact three-step phrasing in a source; it is my summary of the patterns above.
- "Intent-first" in practice means the first screen offers verbs and states of the household (Home / Away / Night, "bedtime", "movie", "who is home", "is anything open") and the room-first map answers "where", with the device inventory last.
- Because users split between task-minded and device-minded, a good design keeps a fast path to the raw device list for the technical adult while defaulting everyone else to intents and rooms.
- "Still work when it fails" argues for the wall display degrading to clock, last-known status with a staleness mark, and physical switches still working. **CVD:** a staleness or offline state should be a shape/label change (e.g. hatched fill plus "offline 5 min"), not a grey-versus-colour difference alone.
- The critique of scenes lacking state awareness suggests showing scenes as states the room is *in* (with a way back), not as fire-and-forget buttons.

### Gaps
- I did not find a Nielsen Norman Group article specific to smart-home dashboards in this pass, nor a primary source for "the best interface is no interface" applied to the home; both are absent from the cited findings.
- No controlled comparison found of room-first versus intent-first versus tile-grid home screens; the evidence is practitioner opinion and vendor direction.
- No case studies from Behance/Dribbble/Medium concept work were retrieved and verified in this pass.
