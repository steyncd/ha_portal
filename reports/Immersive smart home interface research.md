# Live home twins now run in-browser

As of November 2026 the state of the art beyond a Home Assistant dashboard is a live diorama: a glTF model of the real house, rendered with three.js, driven over the Home Assistant WebSocket and redrawn only when state changes. One documented build runs roughly 499,000 triangles on a 2020 iPad Pro in Safari, so this is realistically buildable in a Svelte 5 + Vite PWA on the family's iPhone, 13-inch iPads, MacBook and three-monitor Windows desktop with no recurring cost. Around that core, the web platform now covers a rich non-3D layer on every one of those devices, premium systems supply a proven information architecture (lead with what is active, house modes, one slider per level, an animated energy flow), and experiential design supplies the ideas that keep their value (data lenses, day replay, collections instead of streaks, slow ambient art). What is not buildable is equally clear: WebXR walk-throughs (unsupported on iPhone, iPad and macOS Safari), whole-house or relightable Gaussian splats, dependable web haptics on iOS, camera-based proximity or face adaptation, and per-person coordinates without buying radar hardware. The no-recurring-cost and no-added-inference constraints remove most of the AI-native tier, because cloud speech-to-speech, narrated camera recaps and model-composed dashboards are metered per use or need a GPU; what survives is Home Assistant's deterministic features, the browsers' own speech APIs, and Chrome's on-device Gemini Nano on the Windows desktop alone. The notes conflict on two headline facts and this report leaves both open: whether Apple shipped a home hub in autumn 2026 (two notes cite pages describing an announced and launched Apple HomeView; a third found no post-event confirmation and files the device as rumour), and whether the Khronos Gaussian-splat glTF extension is ratified (the February 2026 release candidate is well sourced; ratification on 3 September 2026 rests on a single secondary source). For a red-green colour-blind primary user the research is unanimous: state travels by shape, position, luminance, motion direction and labels, with blue and orange replacing red and green.

## One architecture won the 3D twin race in 2026

**A wave of young three.js projects converged on the same design.** The long-stale floor3d-card, pinned at three.js r130, was revived in 2026 by forks on r186, and at least five projects that let you draw or load the house inside Home Assistant appeared between June and November: Hollako's 3D Floorplan, Diorama, NeonPlan 3D, NextFloor and Homeslice ([floor3d-card mod](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/floor3d-card-mod-updated-fork-of-the-3d-floor-plan-card-three-js-0-186-lit-3-sunlight-new-editor/1026920), [Hollako](https://github.com/Hollako/Home-Assistant-3D-Floorplan), [Diorama](https://www.simon42.com/diorama-home-assistant-3d-karte/), [NeonPlan 3D](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/neonplan-3d-draw-your-home-in-home-assistant-and-control-it-in-a-live-3d-floor-plan-free-hacs-online-demo/1027224), [NextFloor](https://www.simon42.com/nextfloor-home-assistant-3d-plan/), [Homeslice](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/homeslice-your-home-as-a-live-3d-floor-plan-with-the-editor-built-into-home-assistant/1032185)). Almost all are single-maintainer, weeks to months old, and several are openly AI-assisted, so the notes rate them as idea sources and poor dependencies. What matters is the convergence: **glTF/GLB plus three.js plus the Home Assistant WebSocket, entity ids carried in glTF extras or a side map, render on demand, tap to toggle and long-press for detail.** A bespoke Svelte front end adopts that architecture directly; no card needs embedding. Home Assistant itself shipped no native floor-plan or 3D view in any release from 2025.1 to 2026.11 that the notes read (its public GitHub roadmap was not checked), and its floor and area registries hold no coordinates or polygons, so the app keeps its own geometry keyed by `area_id` ([floor registry](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/home-assistant/core/dev/homeassistant/helpers/floor_registry.py), [area registry](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/home-assistant/core/dev/homeassistant/helpers/area_registry.py)).

**The best-documented build publishes numbers that fit an iPad.** A community project titled *My house as a live 3D dashboard* (thread from 29 September 2026, no public repo) generates the house procedurally in headless Blender, exports a **7.8 MB Draco glTF**, and runs vanilla three.js against the WebSocket with 91 bound entities. On a 2020 iPad Pro 12.9-inch in Safari it renders **about 499,000 triangles and 1,030 draw calls**, loading in about 2.5 seconds on the LAN, by capping textures at 512 px on touch devices (texture memory falls from about 89 MB to 49 MB), holding a 1.5-megapixel render budget, skipping MSAA, rendering on demand and releasing the WebGL context on `pagehide` ([Home Assistant community](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/my-house-as-a-live-3d-dashboard-blender-model-three-js-driven-by-home-assistant/1026746)). Its 59 lamps share a fixed pool of point lights, 14 on desktop and 6 on touch, reassigned to the lamps nearest the camera so shaders never recompile. The same thread records an iOS-only failure: a shader that indexed an array out of range, in a branch that never executes, crashed the iPad's Metal backend seconds after load. The notes hold no measurement on the family's own 13-inch iPads or on any iPhone; the 13-inch iPad size postdates the test device, so the benchmark is conservative guidance, not a guarantee.

**Lighting comes in three price bands.** In ascending GPU cost, projects use emissive-only materials on fixture meshes ([3DHome](https://github.com/lukevink/3DHome)), fake light pools and glow meshes clipped to room polygons so light never bleeds through walls ([Hollako](https://github.com/Hollako/Home-Assistant-3D-Floorplan)), and real lights with shadow maps under a hard budget; one unpooled card draws user reports of lag past six lights ([Floor3D Pro](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/floor3d-pro-card-game-engine-backbone-edition/975682)). Merging static meshes matters as much as compression: Floor3DX cut draw calls from 3,054 to 401 and CPU frame time from 26 ms to 8 ms, and compressed a 30 MB house to about 4 MB ([Floor3DX](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/floor3dx-card-the-3d-floor-plan-card-plus-two-optional-companions-a-2d-snapshot-card-and-a-learning-enabled-scheduler/1029922)). The oldest technique still runs everywhere: pre-render one image per light, stack the layers with `mix-blend-mode: lighten`, and set each layer's opacity to brightness divided by 255 ([Sean Blanchfield](https://seanblanchfield-blog.pages.dev/2024/05/my-smart-home-dashboard)). Savant's TrueImage [shipped] is the photographic cousin: an on photo and an off photo per fixture, cross-faded live as a slider moves, which the notes judge reproducible on the web with nothing more than a phone camera at capture time ([Savant support](https://support.savant.com/help/articles/en_US/Support_Article/Lighting-Service-User-TrueImage/)). Blending per-light baked lightmaps in a shader would give the best quality per unit of GPU cost, but no web implementation was found, so it remains a design proposal.

**Commercial products are deliberately plainer than the hobbyists.** Samsung SmartThings Map View [shipped] and LG ThinQ 3D Home View [shipped in Korea; known only through search summaries of Korean notices] are the only mainstream products found that show a stylised model of the user's own floor plan with live device state ([Samsung support](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10004030/), [LG notice](https://www.lge.co.kr/support/mobile-sw-update-SW20240827414003)). Samsung's pattern is an extruded plan with device icons pinned to rooms, a 2D/3D toggle, stepped rotation, and filters that recolour the map by energy, temperature or lighting; rooms dragged onto the map bring their devices with them. Samsung's generative-AI Map View and appliance-based ambient sensing are [announced] for 2025 to 2026, with no shipping confirmation in the notes ([SmartThings blog](https://blog.smartthings.com/roundups/samsung-smartthings-introduces-new-ai-features-at-unpacked)). None of the commercial products found shows per-person position dots, and the community builds already exceed them in fidelity, which makes the commercial apps interaction references more than visual ones.

**Capturing the house costs time, not money.** Three routes exist. Apple RoomPlan on a LiDAR iPhone or iPad Pro exports clean parametric USDZ that has to pass through Blender to become glTF; every RoomPlan fact in the notes comes from a search summary ([Apple WWDC23](https://developer.apple.com/wwdc23/10192), [three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/usdz-loader-for-three-js/35357)). Hand modelling in Sweet Home 3D or Blender is what nearly every Home Assistant project actually uses. Phone splat capture is the photoreal third route, covered below. The optimisation recipe is glTF-Transform with Meshopt or Draco geometry compression and WebP or KTX2 textures capped at 1024 px (search summary) ([optimisation guide](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/optimize-glb-for-web)). From the published numbers the notes infer a budget of 4 to 8 MB compressed, half a million triangles or fewer, and a few hundred draw calls after merging.

**Live presence is room-level unless the family buys radars.** Only 24 GHz radars built on the HLK-LD2450 give Home Assistant true x/y coordinates: up to three targets, about 6 m of range across a ±60° fan, jitter of hundreds of millimetres, roughly one update per second through ESPHome defaults, and target ids that swap between people ([esp32.co.uk](https://esp32.co.uk/ld2450-zones-home-assistant-coordinates-exclusion-zones/)). The floor3d-card mod became the first 3D card to draw those targets as people in September 2026, and NAMO fuses up to seven radars onto one floor plan ([floor3d-card mod](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/floor3d-card-mod-updated-fork-of-the-3d-floor-plan-card-three-js-0-186-lit-3-sunlight-new-editor/1026920), [NAMO](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/namo-on-device-floorplan-tracking-and-visual-zones-with-esp32-and-ld2450/1017317)). Bluetooth is room-level at best: in the reference build's thread, Bermuda moved a stationary beacon between rooms 33 times in 48 hours ([Home Assistant community](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/my-house-as-a-live-3d-dashboard-blender-model-three-js-driven-by-home-assistant/1026746)). Aqara's FP2 exposes zones only (search summary) ([jpk.io](https://jpk.io/home-automation/aqara-fp2-zones-home-assistant/)), and UWB exists only as forum ideas ([Home Assistant community](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/half-baked-idea-phone-pointing-control-for-ha-via-uwb/1019012)). The buildable design is person chips placed in rooms from presence data Home Assistant already holds, as Homeslice does for people and pets, with anonymous, smoothed blobs only where a radar is installed. That hardware is a one-off purchase the notes do not price, and no accuracy or latency figures for it were published anywhere the notes reached.

## The browser stack clears all four devices; iOS memory is the ceiling

**three.js and Threlte are current and Svelte 5-native.** three.js reached r187 on 30 October 2026; development has moved to `WebGPURenderer` and the TSL shader language, with a WebGL 2 backend built into the same renderer ([Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/threejs-2026-what-changed)). The price is bundle size: a minimal tree-shaken scene is about **121 kB gzip on `WebGLRenderer` versus 192 kB on `WebGPURenderer`**, a CI-bot measurement from April 2026 whose units the notes inferred ([three.js PR #33419](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/pull/33419)). Threlte 8 supports Svelte 5 only, published `@threlte/core` 8.7.0 on 2 November 2026, renders on demand by default and gained WebGPU entry points in September 2026 ([Threlte releases](https://github.com/threlte/threlte/releases), [Threlte render modes](https://threlte.xyz/docs/learn/basics/render-modes)). The notes flag one trap as their own inference: Threlte's documented default shadow type was removed from three.js in r186, so the three.js version should be pinned to one Threlte tests against. Either way the 3D code belongs in a lazily imported route chunk, with render on demand, a clamped device pixel ratio, no MSAA and a context-lost handler, because iOS drops WebGL contexts after backgrounding and under memory pressure (search-snippet reports) ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/three-js-broken-on-ios-17-with-context-lost/58025)).

**WebGPU reaches every family device on paper, and the notes split on trusting it on iOS.** WebGPU ships by default in Chrome and Edge since 113 and in Safari 26.0 on macOS, iOS and iPadOS since 15 September 2025 ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/webgpu-supported-major-browsers), [WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/17333/webkit-features-in-safari-26-0/)). The main iOS PWA tracker lists it as available in installed web apps since 26.0, but no Apple or WebKit statement confirms that ([firt.dev](https://firt.dev/notes/pwa-ios/)). Here the notes disagree in posture. The web-stack note recommends a single `three/webgpu` code path that falls back to WebGL 2 automatically. The 3D note calls WebGPU-only paths risky on iOS, citing WebKit bug reports of GPU-process kills and crashes on iOS 26.0 to 26.1 (search summaries; the bugs were not read individually), and recommends WebGL 2 as the safer default for an installed PWA ([WebKit bug 303203](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=303203)). Both agree on feature detection, a WebGL 2 path and context-loss handling. Which backend is the default on the family's iPhone and iPads is settled only by testing on those devices under current iOS.

**Gaussian splats are a first-class web citizen with a room-sized budget.** three.js r186 added a native `GaussianSplatMesh`, and Spark from World Labs renders splats on WebGL 2 inside an ordinary three.js scene, adding level of detail and streaming in its 2.x line ([Spark docs](https://sparkjs.dev/docs/overview/), [World Labs](https://worldlabs.ai/blog/spark-2.0)); the older GaussianSplats3D is no longer developed ([GaussianSplats3D](https://github.com/mkkellogg/GaussianSplats3D)). Published budgets are **1 to 3 million splats on an iPhone** and 1 to 5 million on a computer ([Spark performance docs](https://sparkjs.dev/docs/performance/)); PlayCanvas measured an iPhone 13 Pro Max at 38 fps on WebGL 2 and 78 fps on WebGPU for one million splats ([PlayCanvas](https://blog.playcanvas.com/new-in-supersplat-webgpu-and-streaming-bring-huge-performance-wins)). A room is roughly 1 to 3 million Gaussians and 15 to 60 MB compressed on third-party estimates, and Scaniverse captures for free where Polycam's full tier costs $13 a month ([mobile capture guide](https://polyvia3d.pages.dev/guides/gaussian-splatting-mobile-capture)). Two limits define the use. A splat is baked lighting: toggling individual lamps is research-grade work (LuxRemix, CVPR 2026; search summary) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15283)). And a whole 14-room interior is tens of millions of Gaussians, unproven on iOS even with streaming. No example of a splat driven by live smart-home state was found, so the notes' hybrid of a photoreal backdrop with invisible proxy meshes for picking is a proposal, strongest for the exterior.

**The Khronos splat standard has an unresolved status.** Both notes that cover it report the same pair of facts. Khronos announced a release candidate of `KHR_gaussian_splatting` in February 2026, open for feedback before ratification ([AEC Magazine](https://aecmag.com/news/khronos-announces-gltf-gaussian-splatting-extension/), [Digital Production](https://digitalproduction.com/2026/02/04/gltf-learns-to-splat-without-losing-the-plot/)). A single secondary site reports that the extension reached ratified status on 3 September 2026 through a registry commit with no Khronos press release ([radiancefields.com](https://radiancefields.com/platforms/the-khronos-group)). Neither note found primary confirmation, so this report treats the extension as a release candidate with an unverified ratification report. The practical effect is small: the base extension defines no compression, and a PWA would load SPZ, SOG or RAD files through Spark regardless.

**Modern CSS covers the non-3D layer on every device.** Same-document view transitions, scroll-driven animations, anchor positioning, popover with invoker commands, `@starting-style`, `linear()` spring easing and `light-dark()` are all in iOS Safari 26 or later and current Chromium ([view transitions](https://web-platform-dx.github.io/web-features-explorer/features/view-transitions), [scroll-driven animations](https://web-platform-dx.github.io/web-features-explorer/features/scroll-driven-animations), [WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/17333/webkit-features-in-safari-26-0/)). Scroll-driven animations became Baseline on 27 October 2026 with Firefox 159. The Chromium-only frontier, which among the family's devices means the Windows desktop alone, is element-scoped view transitions (Chrome 147), nested transition groups, `interpolate-size`, `corner-shape`, HTML-in-Canvas (stable in Chrome 157) and Liquid Glass-style refraction through an SVG filter used as `backdrop-filter`, which degrades to plain blur in Safari ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/element-scoped-view-transitions), [HTML-in-Canvas](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/html-in-canvas), [kube.io](https://kube.io/blog/liquid-glass-css-svg)). For motion, Svelte 5's built-in `Spring` and `Tween` classes cost nothing, Motion's mini `animate()` is 3.2 kB, GSAP is free under a proprietary licence that Webflow can amend, and Rive starts at 222 kB Brotli ([Svelte docs](https://svelte.dev/docs/svelte/svelte-motion), [Motion](https://motion.dev/docs/quick-start), [GSAP licence](https://gsap.com/community/standard-license/), [Rive](https://rive.app/docs/runtimes/runtime-sizes.md)).

**Device APIs divide cleanly between iOS and the Windows desktop.** An installed iOS web app can hold the screen awake (from iOS 18.4), receive push and set badges (from 16.4), synthesise speech, recognise speech through a cloud service, and play WebRTC or MSE video ([firt.dev](https://firt.dev/notes/pwa-ios/)). It cannot vibrate, go fullscreen on iPhone, or use Web Bluetooth, WebXR, Document Picture-in-Picture, Idle Detection or Window Management, and it has no built-in language model. Chromium on the desktop has nearly all of these: the **Window Management API (Chrome and Edge 111, desktop only) is the only way to place windows across the three monitors programmatically**, and Document Picture-in-Picture supplies floating panels ([window management](https://web-platform-dx.github.io/web-features-explorer/features/window-management), [document picture-in-picture](https://web-platform-dx.github.io/web-features-explorer/features/document-picture-in-picture)). Camera tiles work on all four devices through go2rtc, using MSE over WebSocket on Chromium and native HLS on Safari, or through Home Assistant's own `camera/webrtc/offer` signalling ([Home Assistant community](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/my-house-as-a-live-3d-dashboard-blender-model-three-js-driven-by-home-assistant/1026746), [camera/webrtc.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/home-assistant/core/dev/homeassistant/components/camera/webrtc.py)).

**WebXR is absent from every Apple device the family owns.** The WebXR Device API ships in Chrome and on headsets; iPhone, iPad and macOS Safari do not support it ([web-features](https://web-platform-dx.github.io/web-features-explorer/features/webxr-device), [firt.dev](https://firt.dev/notes/pwa-ios/)). Apple's `<model>` element reached iOS, iPadOS and macOS in Safari 27.0, but it is USDZ-only with limited scripting, suited to showing one object and not an interactive twin ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/18325/webkit-features-for-safari-27-0/)). A headset walk-through reusing the same three.js scene is buildable in general; it is not buildable for the stated device list.

## Premium systems lead with what is active, not what exists

**Integrator platforms converged on an active-now home screen.** Josh.ai's AI X OS [shipped, rolling out] opens on a customisable page with a monitor of currently active devices and a scheduled-automation hub; Control4 X4 [shipped] puts favourites and active media sessions on the home screen and adds a Routine Timeline of what the house will do next ([Josh.ai](https://josh.ai/stories/joshai-launches-ai-x-os-debuts-larger-touchscreen-showcases-josh-edge-remote-and-introduces-new-partnerships-at-cedia-expo-2025), [Audio Advice](https://www.audioadvice.com/blogs/expert-advice/control4-x4-os-smarter-more-user-friendly-smart-home-control)). Josh's own research found that people mostly control whole rooms, which led to one slider per level of the hierarchy (house, room, light) and a top list of only the lights that are on ([Josh.ai essay](https://josh.ai/stories/the-hard-thing-about-designing-the-smart-home-lighting-control)). For a home with an alarm and scheduled loads, the notes single out the routine timeline as a trust feature worth borrowing.

**Big tech demoted the device list beneath a brief, a sentence box and modes.** Google's Gemini for Home [shipped, region-limited] adds Ask Home for multi-action sentences and a daily Home Brief, with camera intelligence behind Google Home Premium from $10 a month ([Google](https://blog.google/products/google-nest/gemini-for-home-launch/)). Amazon's Echo Show 8 and 11 [shipped 12 November 2025] change layout as a person approaches, using a sensor suite Amazon calls Omnisense, and switch Home, Away and Night modes with one tap ([About Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/amazon-new-echo-devices-alexa-plus), [About Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/getting-started-echo-show-8-11-alexa-plus-features)). Samsung replaced its Favourites tab with generated insight messages [shipped; search-snippet] ([Samsung Newsroom](https://news.samsung.com/us/samsung-making-home-better-connected-major-smartthings-updates/)). The one-tap house mode maps directly onto a household with a perimeter alarm. Proximity adaptation does not transfer: it needs a camera or radar inside the display, so a web app approximates it only from a Home Assistant presence sensor near the screen and the time since the last touch.

**On Apple's home hub the notes contradict each other.** The 3D note reports that Apple announced a hub named HomeView on 13 October 2026, a 7-inch display at $299 that launched on 13 November 2026 with a Home Modular face, a camera face showing up to nine feeds, a distance-adaptive layout and recognition of up to six faces, and it observes that no floor plan or spatial view is mentioned ([MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/2026/10/13/apple-announces-homeview-smart-home-hub)). The web-stack note separately cites a WebKit post of 13 November 2026 on Safari for Apple HomeView, which advises testing at a 448×364 viewport ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/18608/safari-on-apple-homeview/)). The premium-UX note, researched the same day, confirms only the 13 October event and a shipping HomePod mini 2. It found no post-event confirmation of a hub, records that 9to5Mac's HomePad archive still led with a 9 October preview, and files every interface detail (watch-style faces, Smart Stacks, a price near $350) as [rumour] under the names HomePad and homeOS ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/guides/homepad/), [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/10/02/apples-new-homeos-will-launch-this-month-heres-whats-coming/)). This report does not adjudicate. The names and prices differ between the two accounts, which suggests the third note searched under the rumoured name, but that is a reading and not a verified fact. Nothing in the family's build depends on the answer, and neither account documents a spatial view.

**Liquid Glass is a rulebook worth borrowing and a material worth avoiding.** Apple's design language [shipped] reserves glass for a navigation layer floating above content, forbids glass on glass, tints only primary actions and supplies reduced-transparency, increased-contrast and reduced-motion variants ([Apple WWDC25](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)). Reviewers report poor legibility in translucent areas, including one informal measurement of menu text at 3.8:1 contrast (search-snippet; single informal test) ([Infinum](https://infinum.com/blog/apples-ios-26-liquid-glass-sleek-shiny-and-questionably-accessible/)). For a colour-blind user that is a direct warning against status text on translucent surfaces over photographs.

**Always-on screens work as status boards.** Calm-technology principles ask for the smallest possible amount of attention and use of the periphery ([Calm Tech Institute](https://www.calmtech.institute/calm-tech-principles)). Practitioners agree: a review of an e-ink family display argues for curated information refreshed at the rate it actually changes, and a 2026 Home Assistant thread describes displays whose icons appear only when relevant, while several households in the same thread admit that nobody but the builder looks at the wall screen ([HomeTechHacker](https://hometechhacker.com/trmnl-og-e-ink-dashboard-review-a-focused-battery-powered-family-status-board/), [Home Assistant community](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/are-we-thinking-about-wall-tablets-the-wrong-way/1019690)). Constantly changing tickers are the named anti-pattern. The notes found no quantitative research on item counts or viewing-distance type sizes, so the density guidance is practitioner opinion.

**Energy has one dominant metaphor, and users defend it.** The standard is a small node diagram of solar, battery, grid and home with animated flow, backed by an attribution view. Home Assistant 2025.12 added power and water Sankey cards and 2026.6 added a battery state-of-charge badge ([Home Assistant 2025.12](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2025/12/03/release-202512/), [Home Assistant 2026.6](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2026/06/03/release-20266/)); when 2026.3 replaced the animated flow card with gauges on the electricity page, users asked for it back (search-snippet of forum reports) ([Home Assistant community](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/can-the-energy-flow-card-be-restored-after-2026-3/993203)). The cost is real: animated dots in Power Flow Card Plus drew reports of sustained high CPU use ([Home Assistant community](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/power-flow-card-plus/552326?page=8)). The notes' synthesis stacks three layers (a plain sentence, the live flow, then history) and encodes source by fixed position and icon, direction by arrowheads or moving dashes, and magnitude by line thickness and a number.

**Family displays teach one step at a time, and tile grids teach nothing.** Hearth's routines show a single icon-led step per card with visible progress, which children who cannot read can follow, while parents edit from a phone ([Hearth](https://hearthdisplay.com/blogs/hearth-at-home/the-magic-of-hearth-s-routines)). Google restricts camera AI features to adult administrators (search-snippet), a precedent for hiding camera history and arming controls from child profiles ([Google support](https://support.google.com/googlehome/answer/15542305)). The long-standing critique of tile grids is that uniform tiles mirror how the system stores devices and are nearly indistinguishable at a glance ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/stories/its-time-for-a-complete-home-app-makeover/)), and Home Assistant's own defaults moved room-first, with the generated Home dashboard becoming the default Overview in 2026.2 ([Home Assistant 2026.2](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2026/02/04/release-20262/)). The notes compress the alternative into a glance, control, detail ladder, which is their own summary and not a sourced phrase.

## Home Assistant's AI layer is tool-gated, and the constraints remove most of it

**The platform matured around permissions, not composition.** Across 23 releases Home Assistant added AI Task entities for schema-constrained output (2025.8), a one-click MCP server (2026.10) with a read-only mode, visible tool-call traces in Assist, and an administrator-only tool set that reads logs and history and reloads integrations (2026.11) ([Home Assistant 2025.8](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2025/08/06/release-20258/), [Home Assistant 2026.10](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2026/10/07/release-202610/), [Home Assistant 2026.11](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2026/11/04/release-202611/)). On the interface side its direction is adaptive but deterministic: a Common controls section predicted from past usage and hour of day (2025.10), per-entity card suggestions and automatic sections ([Home Assistant 2025.10](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2025/10/01/release-202510/)). No release shipped a model-composed dashboard, and the notes found no shipped smart-home product that composes its interface with a model at runtime, while noting that their search on that point was limited.

**Everything Assist does is reachable from a custom front end.** The same authenticated WebSocket exposes `assist_pipeline/run` for streamed speech and token deltas, `conversation/process` for text, `logbook/event_stream` and `history/stream` for narrative feeds and replay, and the usage-prediction command behind Common controls ([WebSocket API](https://contributors.home-assistant.io/core/internals/websocket-api/), [Assist pipelines](https://contributors.home-assistant.io/core/internals/assist-pipelines/), [usage prediction](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/home-assistant/core/dev/homeassistant/components/usage_prediction/__init__.py)). Several useful commands are administrator-only, so a read-only narrator and an operator map naturally onto separate Home Assistant users.

**Generative UI settled into three tiers, and the middle one fits Svelte.** Models either pick from pre-built components, emit a declarative spec constrained to a catalogue, or ship HTML into a sandboxed iframe ([CopilotKit](https://copilotkit.ai/blog/the-developer-s-guide-to-generative-ui-in-2026)). Vercel's json-render sits in the declarative tier and ships an official Svelte 5 renderer with streaming partial specs ([json-render](https://github.com/vercel-labs/json-render)). The notes' recommended pattern has the model emit a spec that references entity ids and time ranges only, validated against the real registry, while the client fetches every number itself, so the model cannot misstate the home's state. Google's open-ended approach takes a minute or more per generation, which rules it out for glanceable use ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/generative-ui-a-rich-custom-visual-interactive-user-experience-for-any-prompt/)).

**Narrated vision is where ungrounded models failed in public.** Frigate added structured GenAI review summaries with threat levels in 0.17 and a tool-calling chat agent in 0.18 ([Frigate docs](https://docs.frigate.video/configuration/genai/genai_review), [Frigate 0.18](https://newreleases.io/project/github/blakeblackshear/frigate/release/v0.18.0)). Reviews of Google's Home Brief, which reach the notes through search summaries, found daily narratives that invented people and events ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/813523/gemini-for-home-google-nest-camera-hands-on), [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/google-nest-cam-outdoor-wired-second-gen-review-gemini-just-lied-too-much-2000670298)). The lesson the notes draw is grounding: every sentence links to its clip, identities come only from deterministic recognisers, and low-confidence items are dropped.

**Safety is enforced by tool availability.** A community guide to AI in Home Assistant puts the rule in nine words: "One is an instruction. The other is a control." ([Home Assistant community](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/so-you-want-to-ai-in-home-assistant/1021777)). Researchers demonstrated 14 indirect prompt-injection scenarios against Gemini-powered assistants, including control of home devices, through calendar invitations, emails and shared documents ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12175)). The notes derive a confirm-only list for this household (alarm disarm, the gate and garage in both directions, locks, camera and detection toggles, configuration reloads, and any identity claim about a child) and a client-side pattern: the model proposes an action object, the PWA renders a native confirm sheet, and the WebSocket call runs under the signed-in user's token after a tap. The 2026.11 tool set includes reloading integrations, which this household should exclude or expose read-only.

**Applied to this family, the two hard constraints cut deep.** Recurring cost excludes the natural conversation modes. The notes price Gemini Live at about $0.023 per minute of two-way audio, roughly $7 a month at ten minutes a day, with OpenAI Realtime materially higher on unverified third-party figures ([Gemini API pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing), [third-party roundup](https://aireiter.com/blog/openai-realtime-api-pricing)). The same rule excludes cloud camera recaps, which the notes estimate at $5 to $8 a month on an unverified token assumption, and every commercial camera-AI plan at $10 to $20 a month. The no-inference rule excludes the llama.cpp and Ollama agents Home Assistant now integrates, and local speech-to-text, wherever they would run on the Home Assistant machine; Frigate's local vision models need a GPU with at least 8 GB of memory and Frigate advises against CPU inference ([Frigate GenAI docs](https://docs.frigate.video/configuration/genai/genai_config)). Four routes survive. The reasoning that follows is this report's, applied to facts in the notes.

The first route is deterministic. Home Assistant's built-in agent handles lights, locks, media, timers and to-dos with no language model ([Voice chapter 11](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2025/10/22/voice-chapter-11)); usage prediction is one WebSocket call; the Automation Suggestions integration mines the logbook for repeated manual actions with no model at runtime ([Automation Suggestions](https://github.com/Danm72/home-assistant-automation-suggestions)); and the Activity view already reconstructs the trigger chain behind any change ([Home Assistant 2026.9](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2026/09/02/release-20269/)). A daily digest assembled from rules, statistics deltas and existing camera alerts, phrased by templates, costs nothing. The second route is browser speech. iOS Safari has offered cloud-backed speech recognition since 14.5 and speech synthesis since iOS 7 ([firt.dev](https://firt.dev/notes/pwa-ios/)), and Chrome on the desktop recognises speech on-device from version 139 ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/mdn-api_speechrecognition_processlocally)); recognised text sent to `conversation/process` gives push-to-talk control with no load on the Home Assistant machine. Whether recognition behaves the same inside an installed iOS web app is undocumented in the notes, the MacBook is not covered, and on iOS the audio leaves the house.

The third route is on-device browser AI. Chrome's Prompt API runs Gemini Nano locally with JSON-schema-constrained output from Chrome 148, but only on desktop hardware with 22 GB of free storage and either more than 4 GB of VRAM or 16 GB of RAM, and never on iOS ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/ai/prompt-api)). That makes the Windows desktop the one family device where a free model could phrase a digest or emit a json-render spec. The notes do not test that combination, do not cover Edge or other Chromium builds, and record one mid-2026 blog still describing the API as an origin trial. The fourth route is existing spend. The notes refer to a weekly Gemini narrative and a Cloud Functions path the household already runs; extending it with tap-through evidence and follow-up questions is compatible with the constraint only inside whatever quota is already paid for, which the notes do not document.

## Truthful, slow and growing ideas outlast spectacle

**Award-winning craft points at the tiny knowable world.** The 2025–2026 award circuit is dominated by real-time three.js, scroll-as-camera storytelling and small playable worlds, and the strongest work commits to one idea. Messenger by abeto lets a visitor lap a whole planet in about 30 seconds ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/messenger)). Bruno Simon's rebuilt portfolio is a drivable world on WebGPU and TSL with shared weather, day and night and seasons, and it publishes its performance recipe of instancing, merged geometry, a single palette texture and effects switched off on mobile ([Awwwards case study](https://www.awwwards.com/brunos-portfolio-case-study.html)). The notes sort this vocabulary by lifespan: tiny worlds, one-object focus and rooms-as-alcoves keep daily value, while scroll-driven cinematics are built for a first visit and suit a screen opened ten times a day poorly.

**The diorama earns its place by being a truthful mirror.** In the reference house build the bins physically roll to the pavement and blink on pickup day, an x-ray silhouette shows them through walls, a charging cable appears only when the car is plugged in, and a light streak crosses the PV array in proportion to output ([Home Assistant community](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/my-house-as-a-live-3d-dashboard-blender-model-three-js-driven-by-home-assistant/1026746)). The notes argue that weather, season and time of day matching the window outside is the cheapest source of everyday delight because it changes without anyone touching it, and that children read a diorama before they read a chart. No long-term evaluation of any 3D home dashboard exists; the reference post is weeks old.

**City-builder lenses and a single clock organise the data.** Cities: Skylines shipped 19 info views that recolour one world to show one system at a time ([dev diary](https://beyondsims.com/2015/02/skylines-dev-diary-9-info-views/)), and SimCity's designers described letting the art carry feedback while numbers stay under the surface ([BLDGBLOG](https://bldgblog.com/2013/05/sim-city-an-interview-with-stone-librande/)). Applied to a house, the model desaturates and one system lights up (power, water, security, climate or presence), with the relevant lens opening when an alert fires. The evidence also marks the limit: in a York University study players read critical health information significantly better from a conventional HUD than from in-world displays ([York University](https://www.yorku.ca/mack/ec2018.html)), so alarm and security state belongs in a fixed, high-contrast strip, and sci-fi chrome is the kitsch to avoid. NASA's Open MCT contributes the Time Conductor, one global control that puts every view on screen into live or fixed-timespan mode ([Open MCT](https://nasa.github.io/openmct/docs/static/files/Open_MCT_Users_Guide.pdf)).

**Time becomes an interface through replay.** Frigate's history view offers a scrubbable timeline with a motion-density line and visible recording gaps, UniFi Protect 6.0 added multi-camera scrubbing, and Flightradar24 plays history back at up to 300× ([Frigate docs](https://docs.frigate.video/usage/history), [Ubiquiti](https://blog.ui.com/article/introducing-protect-6-0), [Flightradar24](https://support.fr24.com/support/solutions/articles/3000120423-how-to-view-playback-on-the-flightradar24-website-)). The notes propose a day replay, 24 hours in about 30 seconds inside the diorama with the sun arcing and lights switching room by room, which reuses the live scene under a different clock; NextFloor already ships a time-travel playback of recent activity ([simon42](https://www.simon42.com/nextfloor-home-assistant-3d-plan/)). A Wrapped-style monthly or annual story is the right home for exuberant motion precisely because it is rare. Spotify builds its version in Rive ([Rive](https://rive.app/blog/spotify-used-rive-for-spotify-wrapped-2025)), whose runtime weight argues for CSS and SVG story cards here. Strava's retreat on Flyby after safety concerns is the caution: presence replay stays household-only and opt-in ([Cycling Weekly](https://cyclingweekly.com/news/latest-news/strava-removes-automatic-flybys-after-safety-concerns-472797)).

**Flows and rhythms have cheap, proven forms.** GPU particle fields in the wind-map lineage render up to a million particles at 60 fps ([mapbox/webgl-wind](https://github.com/mapbox/webgl-wind)), and particles travelling along Sankey paths make direction and rate legible at a glance. Calendar heatmaps, horizon charts and 24-hour rings are static between updates; a 2019 study found the radial clock is liked and good for reading what happened at a given hour, but no better for spotting daily patterns ([Waldner et al.](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1907.13534)). Home studies add a constraint. Ambient and artistic feedback is viable in homes ([Rodgers & Bartram](https://peeref.com/works/10368038)), and a recurring finding, which the notes could not pin to a single paper, is that householders want more precise information than such displays give ([Ambienizer paper](https://www3.cs.stonybrook.edu/~mueller/papers/Ambienizer.pdf)). Every artistic layer therefore needs exact numbers one tap away.

**Ambient art and sound from home data exist mostly as installations.** Samsung commissioned real-time TV artworks that respond to time of day, temperature, sun position and wind ([onformative](https://onformative.com/work/samsung-ambient)); a student project turns smart-meter data into a changing e-ink artwork ([Energy Framer](https://a2240772429cc.myportfolio.com/energy-framer)); Powerchord turns appliance power draw into sound designed to fit a domestic soundscape ([TU/e](https://research.tue.nl/en/publications/powerchord-exploring-ambient-audio-feedback-on-energy-use/)). The notes found no verified browser wallpaper driven by a Home Assistant feed apart from the 3D dashboard, so a slow shader painting driven by sun elevation, wind, net power and battery state is a proposal. Sound stays opt-in and time-boxed, and browser autoplay rules require one tap per session.

**For children the research favours collections over streaks.** Seven studies found that showing a broken streak depresses later engagement and that people come to treat the streak itself as the goal ([CU Boulder](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions)). The classic 1973 experiment found that preschoolers promised an award for drawing later drew less ([Lepper et al.](https://typeset.io/pdf/undermining-children-s-intrinsic-interest-with-extrinsic-3qn44yqiou.pdf)). A 2015 study of family housework apps found they lean on extrinsic rewards aimed at individuals instead of the family as a whole ([Bjering et al.](https://dl.ifip.org/hal-01758462v1)), and UNICEF's RITEC-8 framework centres autonomy, competence and social bonds ([UNICEF](https://www.unicef.org/innocenti/projects/responsible-innovation-technology-children)). A residential study adds that making consumption visible created privacy and conflict issues between household members ([SFU](https://summit.sfu.ca/_flysystem/fedora/2022-08/etd21991.pdf)). The notes turn this into a house mascot that thrives with care and is never harmed by lapses, collections that never decay, shared family quests and cosmetic-only unlocks, all proposed concepts with no verified product precedent, plus a rule against per-person consumption attribution on a shared display.

**Restraint is evidence-based, and colour is never the only channel.** The motion types that trigger vestibular symptoms (zooming, spinning, parallax, constant motion near text) are the award-site staples, while fades and small movements are considered safe ([A List Apart](https://alistapart.com/article/designing-safer-web-animation-for-motion-sensitivity)); WCAG 2.3.3 asks that interaction-triggered motion be disableable ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/animation-from-interactions)). Craft writing agrees from the other side: frequent actions get no animation, and delight belongs to rare moments ([Rauno Freiberg](https://rauno.me/craft/interaction-design), [Benji Taylor](https://benji.org/family-values)). The notes resolve this into three motion tiers: ambient (slow drift and cross-fades only), interactive (interruptible springs) and celebration (rare and opt-in). For the colour-blind primary user, the Okabe-Ito palette supplies eight colours built to stay distinct under red-green deficiency, cited from a vendor reference page because the primary papers were not retrieved ([Okabe-Ito reference](https://conceptviz.app/blog/okabe-ito-palette-hex-codes-complete-reference)). The notes pair blue #0072B2 against orange #E69F00 for import versus export, use shape plus position plus a text label for OK, warning and alarm, choose cividis or blue-to-yellow ramps for heatmaps, and caution, as their own unsourced inference, against using that palette's bluish green and vermillion as the only distinction for a critical state.

The 3D note assessed a low-power TV or wall-display browser as a core target and did not assess the MacBook, whereas the brief makes the TV a low priority, so the TV appears once, in the last row. ## Conclusion

The durable asset in this design space is the model file, not the renderer. One glTF authored with entity ids, hinge axes and room membership in its extras feeds the live diorama, every data lens, the day replay, a static snapshot for weak screens and even the 2.5D fallback, while the choice between WebGL 2 and WebGPU, or between raw three.js and Threlte, stays reversible. A scripted Blender build that regenerates the house from a data file is the form of that asset most likely to survive years of renovation and entity churn, and it is the one piece of work no library in the notes does for the family.

The constraints also invert the usual AI roadmap, and the research suggests that is no loss. The public failures of 2025 and 2026 came from models narrating ungrounded state; the designs the notes recommend compute everything deterministically and let a model, where one exists, only phrase and rank. A household that cannot pay per token lands on that architecture by default. The same asymmetry argues for designing by device role instead of scaling one layout: the three-monitor desktop is the only screen with window placement, floating panels, Chromium-only transitions and a free local model, so it is the command centre; the iPads carry the calm, truthful diorama; the iPhone carries fast intent. Three questions decide the details and take an afternoon on the real hardware: which graphics backend survives memory pressure in the installed app on the family's iPhone and iPads, whether speech recognition works in standalone mode, and whether Home Assistant images load as cross-origin textures.
